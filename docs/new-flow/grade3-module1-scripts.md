# Grade 3 · Module 1 — Multiplication and Division with Units of 2, 3, 4, 5 and 10

Click-by-click scripts, one per topic, written in exactly the structure of **"Step By Step Script.docx"**
(adding fractions with different bottom numbers).

> ⚠️ **DRAFT FOR REVIEW.** The structure comes from the two source docs. **Every problem, picture,
> hint, twin and practice number below was written for this draft** and is not in either doc —
> please check each one before it is built.
> (Topic 1's lesson and "your turn" problem reuse the Format doc's own Grade 3 multiplication example.)

**Rules every topic follows** (from the source docs)
- Each block is one screen. The student taps **Next**. No answer is asked for until **Screen 8**.
- One picture per screen. No extra characters talking. Numbers stay small.
- Audio: read the text out loud for students who want it.
- No math words ("array", "commutative", "fact family") on Screens 1–7. They appear as a
  **"math word" sticker** on Screen 9.
- Total taps before practice: **7**. Time: about **90 seconds** before they try it themselves.
- In practice: **if they miss, show the big idea again. Do not replay Screens 1–7 unless they miss twice.**
- **Screen 9 after the twin** uses the twin's own numbers ("Text after the twin" / "Math word sticker after the twin").
- **If the twin is also missed 3 times**, Screen 9 still appears, but as **"Let's keep practicing"** — *"You watched how it works, step by step. The practice problems will help it stick."* — with the big idea and the math word, never "You got it". (Added 2026-09-13 at the founder's request; the source doc does not cover this case.)

Module 1 only uses the units **2, 3, 4, 5 and 10**.

## Topic 1 · Plates of cookies — multiplication means equal groups

**Skill:** multiplication as equal groups · **Grade:** 3 · **Time:** about 90 seconds

**Screen 1 — Hello**
- **Title:** Cookies on plates
- **Picture:** 4 plates in a row. 3 cookies on each plate.
- **Text:** There are 4 plates. Each plate has 3 cookies. How many cookies are there?
- **Button:** Next
- **Note for designer:** Do not ask for an answer yet.

**Screen 2 — Why we don't count one by one**
- **Title:** Counting one by one is slow
- **Picture:** The same cookies, with a small number appearing on each one: 1, 2, 3 … 12.
- **Text:** You could count every cookie. That works, but it is slow, and it is easy to lose your place.
- **Button:** Next

**Screen 3 — The one big idea**
- **Title:** The big idea
- **Picture:** The same 4 plates, sitting still.
- **Text:** When every group has the same number, count by groups. Multiplication means equal groups.
- **Button:** Next
- **Note:** This sentence should appear again later if they get a practice problem wrong.

**Screen 4 — Find the groups**
- **Title:** How many groups? How many in each?
- **Picture:** A ring is drawn around each plate, one at a time. Label under the rings: "4 groups". One plate glows with the label "3 in each".
- **Text:** There are 4 plates, so there are 4 groups. Each plate has 3 cookies, so there are 3 in each group.
- **Button:** Next
- **Motion:** The rings should appear one plate at a time. Do not skip this.

**Screen 5 — Count by groups**
- **Title:** Count by 3s
- **Picture:** The plates light up one at a time. A running total appears above each: 3, 6, 9, 12.
- **Text:** Count one whole plate at a time: 3, 6, 9, 12.
- **Button:** Next

**Screen 6 — Write it down**
- **Title:** 4 groups of 3
- **Picture:** Under the plates: **4 × 3 = 12**. An arrow from 4 says "groups". An arrow from 3 says "in each".
- **Text:** 4 groups of 3 make 12. We write 4 × 3 = 12. The first number is how many groups. The second number is how many in each group.
- **Button:** Next

**Screen 7 — Watch out**
- **Title:** One thing not to do
- **Picture:** A small "wrong" card: 4 × 3 = 7 with an X. Next to it, the right card: 4 × 3 = 12 with a check.
- **Text:** Don't add the numbers. 4 + 3 = 7 is one plate of 3 and 4 more cookies. 4 × 3 means 4 whole plates of 3.
- **Button:** Next

**Screen 8 — Your turn**
- **Title:** Now you try
- **Picture:** 3 empty plates. The student can tap a plate to put cookies on it.
- **Problem:** 3 plates. 5 cookies on each plate. How many cookies?
- **Prompt:** Find how many groups and how many in each. Then count by groups.
- **Answer box:** student types 15
- **Buttons:** Check
- **Hint after 1 miss:** "How many plates are there? How many cookies are on each plate?"
- **Hint after 2 misses:** "There are 3 groups of 5. Count by 5s, one plate at a time: 5, 10 …"
- **If still wrong:** Show the worked steps for this problem, then give a new twin: 5 plates, 2 cookies on each.

**Screen 9 — If they get Screen 8 right**
- **Title:** You got it
- **Text:** You counted by groups instead of one by one. That is multiplication.
- **Math word sticker:** "Equal groups = multiplication. 3 × 5 means 3 groups of 5."
- **Text after the twin:** You counted by groups instead of one by one. That is multiplication.
- **Math word sticker after the twin:** "Equal groups = multiplication. 5 × 2 means 5 groups of 2."
- **Button:** Keep practicing

**Practice**

| Problem | Why it's here |
|---|---|
| 1. 2 plates, 3 cookies on each | Almost a copy of the lesson |
| 2. 5 plates, 2 cookies on each | Same idea, new numbers |
| 3. 4 bags, 5 marbles in each | Still "equal groups", new objects |
| 4. 10 plates, 2 cookies on each | A little harder |
| 5. Word problem: "Sam has 3 packs of stickers. Each pack has 4 stickers. How many stickers?" | Same math in a story |

Answers: 6 · 10 · 20 · 20 · 12

## Topic 2 · Rows of chairs — equal rows

**Skill:** arrays (equal rows) · **Grade:** 3 · **Time:** about 90 seconds

**Screen 1 — Hello**
- **Title:** Chairs in a hall
- **Picture:** Chairs set out neatly in 3 rows, 5 chairs in each row.
- **Text:** The chairs are set out in 3 rows. Each row has 5 chairs. How many chairs are there?
- **Button:** Next
- **Note for designer:** Do not ask for an answer yet.

**Screen 2 — Why rows help**
- **Title:** A jumble is hard to count
- **Picture:** On the left, the same 15 chairs in a messy pile. On the right, the neat rows.
- **Text:** When things are jumbled, you have to count every one, and it is easy to miss some. Neat rows are easier.
- **Button:** Next

**Screen 3 — The one big idea**
- **Title:** The big idea
- **Picture:** The neat rows, sitting still.
- **Text:** When every row has the same number, count the rows, count one row, then multiply.
- **Button:** Next
- **Note:** This sentence should appear again later if they get a practice problem wrong.

**Screen 4 — Count the rows**
- **Title:** How many rows?
- **Picture:** Each row lights up in turn, with 1, 2, 3 appearing at its left end.
- **Text:** Rows go across. There are 3 rows.
- **Button:** Next
- **Motion:** Light the rows one at a time. Do not skip this.

**Screen 5 — Count one row**
- **Title:** How many in a row?
- **Picture:** Only the top row glows. Its chairs are numbered 1 to 5.
- **Text:** Every row is the same, so count just one. Each row has 5 chairs.
- **Button:** Next

**Screen 6 — Count by rows**
- **Title:** 3 rows of 5
- **Picture:** Row by row lights up with a running total: 5, 10, 15. Under the chairs: **3 × 5 = 15**.
- **Text:** Count one whole row at a time: 5, 10, 15. 3 rows of 5 is 15. We write 3 × 5 = 15.
- **Button:** Next

**Screen 7 — Watch out**
- **Title:** One thing not to do
- **Picture:** A small "wrong" card: rows of 5, 5 and 4 chairs (one chair missing) labelled 3 × 5, with an X. Next to it, the right card: three full rows of 5, with a check.
- **Text:** Every row must have the same number. If one row is missing a chair, it is not 3 × 5 any more.
- **Button:** Next

**Screen 8 — Your turn**
- **Title:** Now you try
- **Picture:** An empty garden bed with 4 rows of spaces, 3 spaces in each row. The student can tap a space to plant a tomato.
- **Problem:** A garden has 4 rows. Each row has 3 tomato plants. How many plants?
- **Prompt:** Count the rows. Count one row. Then multiply.
- **Answer box:** student types 12
- **Buttons:** Check
- **Hint after 1 miss:** "How many rows are there? How many plants are in one row?"
- **Hint after 2 misses:** "There are 4 rows of 3. Count by 3s, one row at a time: 3, 6 …"
- **If still wrong:** Show the worked steps for this problem, then give a new twin: 2 rows, 5 plants in each row.

**Screen 9 — If they get Screen 8 right**
- **Title:** You got it
- **Text:** You counted the rows and one row, then multiplied. That is the whole trick.
- **Math word sticker:** "Equal rows = an array."
- **Text after the twin:** You counted the rows and one row, then multiplied. That is the whole trick.
- **Math word sticker after the twin:** "Equal rows = an array."
- **Button:** Keep practicing

**Practice**

| Problem | Why it's here |
|---|---|
| 1. A muffin tray: 3 rows, 4 muffins in each row | Almost a copy of the lesson |
| 2. A building: 5 rows of windows, 2 windows in each row | Same idea, new numbers |
| 3. A sheet of stamps: 4 rows, 5 stamps in each row | Still "equal rows" |
| 4. A box of chocolates: 5 rows, 5 in each row | A little harder |
| 5. Word problem: "The class sits in 4 rows. There are 4 desks in each row. How many desks?" | Same math in a story |

## Topic 3 · Turn the tray — the order does not change the total

**Skill:** commutative property of multiplication · **Grade:** 3 · **Time:** about 90 seconds

**Screen 1 — Hello**
- **Title:** A tray of muffins
- **Picture:** A tray with 2 rows of muffins, 5 muffins in each row.
- **Text:** This tray has 2 rows of 5 muffins. What happens if we turn the tray around?
- **Button:** Next
- **Note for designer:** Do not ask for an answer yet.

**Screen 2 — It looks different**
- **Title:** It looks like a new problem
- **Picture:** The same tray turned sideways: now 5 rows of 2 muffins.
- **Text:** After turning, there are 5 rows of 2. It looks different. Did the number of muffins change?
- **Button:** Next

**Screen 3 — The one big idea**
- **Title:** The big idea
- **Picture:** The tray, both ways, side by side.
- **Text:** Turning the rows around does not change how many there are. 2 × 5 and 5 × 2 make the same total.
- **Button:** Next
- **Note:** This sentence should appear again later if they get a practice problem wrong.

**Screen 4 — Count the first way**
- **Title:** 2 rows of 5
- **Picture:** The tray as it started. Rows light up: 5, 10. Label: **2 × 5 = 10**.
- **Text:** Count by rows: 5, 10. 2 × 5 = 10.
- **Button:** Next

**Screen 5 — Turn it**
- **Title:** Turn the tray
- **Picture:** The tray slowly turns a quarter turn. No muffin is added or taken away.
- **Text:** Watch the tray turn. Nothing falls off and nothing is added.
- **Button:** Next
- **Motion:** The turn should animate. Do not skip this.

**Screen 6 — Count the new way**
- **Title:** 5 rows of 2
- **Picture:** The turned tray. Rows light up: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10. Label: **5 × 2 = 10**.
- **Text:** Count by rows: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10. 5 × 2 = 10. Same muffins, same total.
- **Button:** Next

**Screen 7 — Watch out**
- **Title:** One thing not to do
- **Picture:** A small "wrong" card: 5 − 2 = 2 − 5 with an X. Next to it, the right card: 5 × 2 = 2 × 5 with a check.
- **Text:** This turn-around trick works for times. It does not work for take away. 5 − 2 is not the same as 2 − 5.
- **Button:** Next

**Screen 8 — Your turn**
- **Title:** Now you try
- **Picture:** 4 rows of 10 dots, with a "turn" button the student can tap to spin it.
- **Problem:** 4 × 10 = 40. What is 10 × 4?
- **Prompt:** Turn the rows around. Does the total change?
- **Answer box:** student types 40
- **Buttons:** Check
- **Hint after 1 miss:** "Tap turn. Were any dots added or taken away?"
- **Hint after 2 misses:** "10 × 4 is the same dots turned around, so it has the same total as 4 × 10."
- **If still wrong:** Show the worked steps for this problem, then give a new twin: 3 × 5 = 15. What is 5 × 3?

**Screen 9 — If they get Screen 8 right**
- **Title:** You got it
- **Text:** You turned the rows around and the total stayed the same. That is the whole trick.
- **Math word sticker:** "Turn-around facts = the commutative property."
- **Text after the twin:** You turned the rows around and the total stayed the same. That is the whole trick.
- **Math word sticker after the twin:** "Turn-around facts = the commutative property."
- **Button:** Keep practicing

**Practice**

| Problem | Why it's here |
|---|---|
| 1. 2 × 4 = 8. What is 4 × 2? | Almost a copy of the lesson |
| 2. 3 × 10 = 30. What is 10 × 3? | Same idea, new numbers |
| 3. 5 × 4 = 20. What is 4 × 5? | Still "turn it around" |
| 4. 5 × __ = 2 × 5. What number goes in the blank? | A little harder |
| 5. Word problem: "Ana puts 3 stickers on each of 4 pages. She has 12 stickers. Ben puts 4 stickers on each of 3 pages. How many stickers does Ben have?" | Same math in a story |

Answers: 8 · 30 · 20 · 2 · 12

## Topic 4 · Counting by 2s, 5s and 10s

**Skill:** skip counting as multiplication (2, 5, 10) · **Grade:** 3 · **Time:** about 90 seconds

**Screen 1 — Hello**
- **Title:** Socks on the line
- **Picture:** A washing line with 6 pairs of socks pegged up.
- **Text:** There are 6 pairs of socks on the line. Each pair has 2 socks. How many socks are there?
- **Button:** Next
- **Note for designer:** Do not ask for an answer yet.

**Screen 2 — Why we don't count one by one**
- **Title:** One by one is slow
- **Picture:** A number appears on every single sock: 1, 2, 3 … 12.
- **Text:** You could count every sock. But socks come in pairs, so there is a faster way.
- **Button:** Next

**Screen 3 — The one big idea**
- **Title:** The big idea
- **Picture:** The 6 pairs, sitting still.
- **Text:** When the groups are the same size, jump by that size. Each jump counts a whole group.
- **Button:** Next
- **Note:** This sentence should appear again later if they get a practice problem wrong.

**Screen 4 — Jump by 2s**
- **Title:** Jump by 2s
- **Picture:** A number line from 0 to 12 under the socks. One jump of 2 for each pair, landing on 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12.
- **Text:** Each pair is a jump of 2: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12.
- **Button:** Next
- **Motion:** Each jump should animate as its pair of socks lights up. Do not skip this.

**Screen 5 — Count the jumps**
- **Title:** How many jumps?
- **Picture:** The same jumps, numbered 1 to 6 above the arcs. Label: **6 × 2 = 12**.
- **Text:** We made 6 jumps of 2, and we landed on 12. 6 × 2 = 12.
- **Button:** Next

**Screen 6 — 5s and 10s work the same way**
- **Title:** Hands and bundles
- **Picture:** Top: 3 hands, with 5, 10, 15 appearing. Bottom: 4 bundles of 10 straws, with 10, 20, 30, 40 appearing.
- **Text:** Hands count by 5s: 5, 10, 15. Bundles of ten count by 10s: 10, 20, 30, 40.
- **Button:** Next

**Screen 7 — Watch out**
- **Title:** One thing not to do
- **Picture:** A small "wrong" card: 4 hands → 5, 10, 15, with an X. Next to it, the right card: 4 hands → 5, 10, 15, 20, with a check.
- **Text:** Don't stop too early. 4 hands means 4 jumps. Hold up a finger for each jump.
- **Button:** Next

**Screen 8 — Your turn**
- **Title:** Now you try
- **Picture:** 7 hands, and an empty number line from 0 to 40 the student can tap to make jumps.
- **Problem:** 7 hands. How many fingers?
- **Prompt:** Jump by 5s. Count one jump for each hand.
- **Answer box:** student types 35
- **Buttons:** Check
- **Hint after 1 miss:** "Count by 5s. Hold up one finger for each hand you count."
- **Hint after 2 misses:** "5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30 is 6 hands. Make one more jump."
- **If still wrong:** Show the worked steps for this problem, then give a new twin: 6 bundles of 10 straws. How many straws?

**Screen 9 — If they get Screen 8 right**
- **Title:** You got it
- **Text:** You jumped a whole group at a time. Counting by jumps is multiplying.
- **Math word sticker:** "Skip counting by 5 = the 5 times table."
- **Text after the twin:** You jumped a whole group at a time. Counting by jumps is multiplying.
- **Math word sticker after the twin:** "Skip counting by 10 = the 10 times table."
- **Button:** Keep practicing

**Practice**

| Problem | Why it's here |
|---|---|
| 1. 4 pairs of shoes. How many shoes? | Almost a copy of the lesson |
| 2. 5 bundles of 10 straws. How many straws? | Same idea, new numbers |
| 3. 8 hands. How many fingers? | Still "jump by groups" |
| 4. 9 pairs of socks. How many socks? | A little harder |
| 5. Word problem: "A nickel is worth 5 cents. Mia has 6 nickels. How many cents does she have?" | Same math in a story |

## Topic 6 · Share the apples fairly — how many in each group

**Skill:** division as equal sharing · **Grade:** 3 · **Time:** about 90 seconds

**Screen 1 — Hello**
- **Title:** Sharing apples
- **Picture:** A basket of 12 apples and 3 friends standing behind 3 empty plates.
- **Text:** 12 apples are shared fairly by 3 friends. How many apples does each friend get?
- **Button:** Next
- **Note for designer:** Do not ask for an answer yet.

**Screen 2 — What fair means**
- **Title:** Fair means the same
- **Picture:** A share that is not fair: plates with 6, 4 and 2 apples, and a sad face on the last friend.
- **Text:** If one friend gets 6 and another gets 2, that isn't fair. Every friend must get the same number.
- **Button:** Next

**Screen 3 — The one big idea**
- **Title:** The big idea
- **Picture:** The basket of 12 and the 3 empty plates, sitting still.
- **Text:** Division starts with the total and shares it into equal groups. Here we know how many groups, and we find how many in each.
- **Button:** Next
- **Note:** This sentence should appear again later if they get a practice problem wrong.

**Screen 4 — Deal them out**
- **Title:** One for you, one for you …
- **Picture:** Apples move from the basket, one to each plate in turn.
- **Text:** Give one apple to each friend. Then go round again.
- **Button:** Next
- **Motion:** The apples should move one at a time, round and round. Do not skip this.

**Screen 5 — Until none are left**
- **Title:** Keep going
- **Picture:** The basket is empty. Each plate has 4 apples.
- **Text:** Keep going until the basket is empty. Now each friend has 4 apples. It is fair.
- **Button:** Next

**Screen 6 — Write it down**
- **Title:** 12 shared by 3
- **Picture:** Under the plates: **12 ÷ 3 = 4**. An arrow from 12 says "total". An arrow from 3 says "friends". An arrow from 4 says "each".
- **Text:** 12 shared by 3 friends is 4 each. We write 12 ÷ 3 = 4. The first number is the total.
- **Button:** Next

**Screen 7 — Watch out**
- **Title:** One thing not to do
- **Picture:** A small "wrong" card: 3 ÷ 12 with an X. Next to it, the right card: 12 ÷ 3 = 4 with a check.
- **Text:** The first number is the total. Don't flip it to 3 ÷ 12. You are sharing 12 apples, not 3.
- **Button:** Next

**Screen 8 — Your turn**
- **Title:** Now you try
- **Picture:** 10 cookies and 2 empty plates. The student can tap a cookie to move it onto a plate.
- **Problem:** 10 cookies are shared fairly by 2 friends. How many does each friend get?
- **Prompt:** Share them out one at a time until none are left.
- **Answer box:** student types 5
- **Buttons:** Check
- **Hint after 1 miss:** "Give one cookie to each friend. Then go round again until none are left."
- **Hint after 2 misses:** "After 4 rounds, each plate has 4 cookies and 2 are left. Give one more to each friend."
- **If still wrong:** Show the worked steps for this problem, then give a new twin: 15 stickers shared fairly by 5 friends.

**Screen 9 — If they get Screen 8 right**
- **Title:** You got it
- **Text:** You shared the total fairly. That is division.
- **Math word sticker:** "÷ means share into equal groups."
- **Text after the twin:** You shared the total fairly. That is division.
- **Math word sticker after the twin:** "÷ means share into equal groups."
- **Button:** Keep practicing

**Practice**

| 2. 20 pencils shared by 4 kids. How many each? | Same idea, new numbers |

| 4. 18 grapes shared by 3 friends. How many each? | A little harder |

## Topic 7 · How many bags can we fill? — how many groups

**Skill:** division as making groups of a given size · **Grade:** 3 · **Time:** about 90 seconds

**Screen 1 — Hello**
- **Title:** Filling bags
- **Picture:** 12 apples on a table and a stack of empty bags.
- **Text:** There are 12 apples. We put 3 apples in each bag. How many bags can we fill?
- **Button:** Next
- **Note for designer:** Do not ask for an answer yet.

**Screen 2 — A different question**
- **Title:** This time it's different
- **Picture:** One bag holding 3 apples, and question marks where more bags might go.
- **Text:** When we shared apples, we knew how many friends. This time we know how many go in each bag. We don't know how many bags.
- **Button:** Next

**Screen 3 — The one big idea**
- **Title:** The big idea
- **Picture:** The 12 apples, sitting still.
- **Text:** Division can also mean: how many groups of this size can we make?
- **Button:** Next
- **Note:** This sentence should appear again later if they get a practice problem wrong.

**Screen 4 — Make one group**
- **Title:** Fill one bag
- **Picture:** A ring is drawn around 3 apples, and they drop into a bag.
- **Text:** Take 3 apples and put them in a bag. That is one bag.
- **Button:** Next
- **Motion:** The ring and the drop should animate. Do not skip this.

**Screen 5 — Until none are left**
- **Title:** Keep filling
- **Picture:** Rings around 3 more, 3 more, 3 more, until the table is empty. 4 full bags.
- **Text:** Keep making bags of 3 until no apples are left.
- **Button:** Next

**Screen 6 — Count the bags**
- **Title:** How many bags?
- **Picture:** The 4 bags numbered 1 to 4. Under them: **12 ÷ 3 = 4**.
- **Text:** We filled 4 bags. 12 ÷ 3 = 4, because 4 bags of 3 make 12.
- **Button:** Next

**Screen 7 — Watch out**
- **Title:** One thing not to do
- **Picture:** A small "wrong" card: "3 bags" with an X. Next to it, the right card: "4 bags" with a check.

- **Problem:** 15 stickers. 5 stickers go in each pack. How many packs?

- **Answer box:** student types 3
- **Buttons:** Check

## Topic 8 · The missing number — multiplication and division are partners

**Skill:** relating multiplication and division (unknown factor) · **Grade:** 3 · **Time:** about 90 seconds

**Screen 1 — Hello**
- **Title:** Setting out chairs
- **Picture:** 20 chairs in a stack and 4 empty rows marked on the floor.
- **Text:** 20 chairs are set out in 4 equal rows. How many chairs go in each row?
- **Button:** Next
- **Note for designer:** Do not ask for an answer yet.

**Screen 2 — One number is missing**
- **Title:** A number is missing
- **Picture:** The sentence **4 × ? = 20**, with a glowing box where the question mark is.
- **Text:** We know there are 4 rows. We know there are 20 chairs. The number in each row is missing.
- **Button:** Next

**Screen 3 — The one big idea**
- **Title:** The big idea
- **Picture:** The 4 empty rows and 20 chairs, sitting still.
- **Text:** Multiplication and division are partners. A times fact you know can find a missing number.
- **Button:** Next
- **Note:** This sentence should appear again later if they get a practice problem wrong.

**Screen 4 — Try the fact**
- **Title:** Count by 4s
- **Picture:** A number line from 0 to 20 with jumps of 4, landing on 4, 8, 12, 16, 20. The jumps are numbered 1 to 5.
- **Text:** Count by 4s until you reach 20: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20. That is 5 jumps.
- **Button:** Next
- **Motion:** Each jump should animate. Do not skip this.

**Screen 5 — Fill the gap**
- **Title:** The missing number is 5
- **Picture:** The chairs fill the 4 rows, 5 in each row. The sentence becomes **4 × 5 = 20**.
- **Text:** 4 rows of 5 make 20. So each row has 5 chairs.
- **Button:** Next

**Screen 6 — Partner facts**
- **Title:** One picture, four facts
- **Picture:** A triangle with 20 at the top and 4 and 5 at the bottom corners. Beside it: 4 × 5 = 20, 5 × 4 = 20, 20 ÷ 4 = 5, 20 ÷ 5 = 4.
- **Text:** The same three numbers make four facts. If 4 × 5 = 20, then 20 ÷ 4 = 5.
- **Button:** Next

**Screen 7 — Watch out**
- **Title:** One thing not to do
- **Picture:** A small "wrong" card: 5 ÷ 20 = 4 with an X. Next to it, the right card: 20 ÷ 5 = 4 with a check.
- **Text:** The biggest number is the total. It is the answer when you multiply, and it comes first when you divide.
- **Button:** Next

**Screen 8 — Your turn**
- **Title:** Now you try
- **Picture:** A triangle with 30 at the top, 3 in one bottom corner, and an empty corner the student can fill.
- **Problem:** 3 × ? = 30
- **Prompt:** Use a times fact you know to find the missing number.
- **Answer box:** student types 10
- **Buttons:** Check
- **Hint after 1 miss:** "3 groups of what number make 30?"
- **Hint after 2 misses:** "Count by 10s three times: 10, 20, 30. What did you count by?"
- **If still wrong:** Show the worked steps for this problem, then give a new twin: 5 × ? = 15.

**Screen 9 — If they get Screen 8 right**
- **Title:** You got it
- **Text:** You used a times fact to find the missing number. That is the whole trick.
- **Math word sticker:** "3 numbers, 4 facts = a fact family."
- **Text after the twin:** You used a times fact to find the missing number. That is the whole trick.
- **Math word sticker after the twin:** "Numbers that go together, like 5, 3 and 15 = a fact family."
- **Button:** Keep practicing
